# Weighted versions of bodyweight moves (the owner's item 31)

Agreed on 2026-10-11; built in Maintenance 30 (round L).

## The problem

Standing Calf Raise, Reverse Lunge and Bulgarian Split Squat were bodyweight-only in the catalog,
and the catalog had no loaded lunge, no loaded split squat and no standing calf machine. A weight
typed on a bodyweight move carries forward (Maintenance 21), but the plan itself never moved a lifter
off a bodyweight move they had outgrown. The owner asked for "weighted versions of bodyweight moves
at the gym (a dumbbell reverse lunge, a standing calf raise machine)".

## What the research says

Every PMID below was read on PubMed through E-utilities on 2026-10-10. On 2026-10-11 the PMIDs the
design rests on were checked again for their first author, year and title. Full texts were read
where marked *.

- **Load does not decide muscle growth, within limits.** Sets must end near failure and the load
  must be at least about 30% of the one-rep max, which is sets of up to about 30-35 reps:
  - Schoenfeld 2017 (PMID 28834797): 21 trials, every set to failure;
  - Lopez 2021 (33433148): network meta-analysis, 28 studies;
  - Currier 2023 (37414459): Bayesian network meta-analysis;
  - Carvalho 2022 (35015560): volume-load matched;
  - the ACSM 2026 position stand* (Currier 2026, 41843416): growth was unaffected from 30% to
    100% of the max, and sets ending 2-3 reps short of failure are enough.

  In every trial, one-rep-max strength gains were smaller at light loads.

- **At a light load, the effort is what grows the muscle.**
  - Knee extensions at 30% of the max, taken to failure (about 34 reps), grew the quadriceps 7.8%,
    as much as heavy sets did.
  - The same load stopped at about 20 reps grew them 2.8%, not a significant change.
  - At 80% of the max, stopping short made no difference (Lasevicius 2022, 31895290).
  - Below about 20-30% of the max the trials disagree (Lasevicius 2018, 29564973; Buckner 2020,
    31486720; Jessee 2018, 30386254). Reviews place the floor near 30% (Weakley 2023, 37171517;
    Schoenfeld 2021*, 33671664).
- **Long sets cost time, discomfort, recovery and an accurate reserve.** None of these costs builds
  muscle.
  - Time and discomfort: sets of 26 reps took 158 s against 50 s for 8, and felt 7.8 against 4.8
    out of 10 (Fisher 2018, 30498645).
  - Recovery: at matched volume load, lighter loads left the trained leg still unrecovered at
    48 hours (Farrow 2021, 32401690).
  - Reserve: lifters judge their reps in reserve worse as a set runs long, about 0.47 rep of error
    for each rep past 12 against 0.06 below 12 (Halperin 2022, 34542869, an exploratory
    meta-regression).
  - So a 30-rep bodyweight set aimed at 2 in reserve can stop far short. That is the light-load,
    stopped-short case that grew little in Lasevicius 2022.
- **A calf raise on two legs at bodyweight is far too light.** The 25th percentile of healthy adults
  reached 39-67 reps to fatigue, depending on age and sex (Monteiro 2017, 28709587).
  - Loaded calf raises of 20-30 reps grew the calves as much as 6-10 (Schoenfeld 2020*, 32342648).
  - Standing raises, knees straight, grew the calf far more than seated ones: lateral
    gastrocnemius 12.4% against 1.7% (Kinoshita 2023, 38156065). So the loaded version of a
    standing calf raise is a standing machine or a leg-press raise, never the seated machine.
- **A loaded lunge or split squat is the same movement.**
  - Forward lunges with up to half of body mass added kept their joint angles (Riemann 2012,
    22889652).
  - A loaded rear-foot-elevated split squat matched the back squat for strength gains (Speirs 2016,
    26200193).
  - Equipment type changes growth little. Strength is specific to what is trained (Haugen 2023,
    37582807), so the loaded version keeps the move: a lunge stays one leg at a time.
- **Inside its range, loading a move adds nothing for muscle.**
  - Push-ups matched to 40% of the bench max grew the chest and triceps as the bench press did
    (Kikuchi 2017*, 29541130).
  - Loading inside the range adds set-up and balance, not growth.
- **No trial compares a bodyweight lunge, split squat or calf raise with its loaded version.** The
  rule below applies the findings above; the threshold is ACSM 2009's convention for adding load
  (19204579), not a trial-tested number.

## The design that follows

1. **The loaded versions.** Three entries join the catalog, each the same movement loaded:
   - Dumbbell Reverse Lunge;
   - Dumbbell Bulgarian Split Squat, because the lower day picks the Bulgarian split squat at both
     the gym and at home;
   - Standing Calf Raise Machine, a new piece of equipment ("Standing calf raise machine") that a
     place can list.

   The Leg Press Calf Raise, already in the catalog, also counts as a loaded standing calf raise.

2. **Bodyweight while it fits.** A bodyweight move keeps its place while bodyweight still fits its
   reps. With no history it starts at bodyweight: the first set measures it (Nuzzo 2024*, 37792272:
   reps at a given load vary most at light loads).
3. **Outgrown, it is loaded.** A move is outgrown by either of two things:
   - a working set at bodyweight that goes a rep past the top of its range at the reserve the plan
     asked for (reps plus reps in reserve, less the planned reserve, at or above the top plus one; a
     set with no reserve logged counts as taken to failure);
   - a weight the lifter added to it, which says bodyweight alone was too light.

   Once a move is outgrown, its loaded version takes its place wherever the place has one: at the
   gym, and at home when it has dumbbells and a bench. The same rule holds for every goal and
   every level: training status does not change the evidence above (Schoenfeld 2015, 25853914;
   Morton 2016, 27174923; Halperin 2022).

4. **It stays outgrown until bodyweight is hard again.** Sets at bodyweight inside the range say
   nothing either way, because the plan's own range stops them at its top. A hotel's bodyweight split
   squats done as planned never undo the gym's loaded ones. Only a workout where no set at bodyweight
   reached the bottom of its range at the planned reserve brings the bodyweight move back.
5. **A calf raise on two legs is loaded wherever it can be**, with no history needed (Monteiro
   2017). Where a place has no loaded calf raise, as at the default home, it stays at bodyweight.
6. **The plan says why, once, at the change.** "Why this workout" names it, for example: "Dumbbell
   Bulgarian Split Squat in place of the Bulgarian Split Squat: at bodyweight you went past the top
   of its reps, so it is loaded now." The line shows until the loaded version is done at that place.
   A lifter who does the bodyweight move at home and the loaded one at the gym is told once.

   The line is not shown:
   - for an entry a swap put in, which has the swap's own line;
   - where the bodyweight move could not stand: a disliked move, or one the place's equipment or the
     lifter's limits rule out.

7. **The lifter's own choices stand.**
   - A preferred exercise still wins its slot outright.
   - A lasting swap back to the bodyweight move keeps it.
   - A lasting swap away from the bodyweight move, to another exercise, takes its loaded versions
     away too: the same movement. The swap stands as before, loaded or not.
   - Where no loaded version is, a weight typed on the bodyweight move keeps carrying forward there
     (Maintenance 21).

## Questions the evidence leaves open, settled by the app's conventions

The research named five questions only the owner could settle on preference. Each was settled by
the evidence above together with the app's existing conventions, so none needed asking:

1. A first exposure is measured at bodyweight (point 2).
2. When several loaded versions fit, the existing ranking chooses: equipment type changes growth
   little (Haugen 2023; ACSM 2026).
3. The plan makes the change and says why; a swap back stays available (points 6 and 7).
4. The threshold is one rep past the top of the range at the planned reserve, in one workout: ACSM
   2009's convention adds load once a lifter can do one or two reps past the target, and one is the
   earlier of the two. The progression engine already tells a bodyweight lift that reaches the top
   of its range to "add reps or load the movement"; past the top, the plan now loads it.
5. A strength-focused lifter follows the same rule: strength-role sets have low ranges, so they load
   sooner.

## Uncertainties

- No direct trial compares bodyweight and loaded lunges, split squats or calf raises.
- No reps-to-max table exists for lunges or calf raises, so the threshold is approximate.
- The way back to bodyweight (no set at bodyweight reaching the bottom of its range) is a judgement
  drawn from how the plan's range caps the reps a lifter logs; no study set it.
- The reserve-accuracy figures come from an exploratory meta-regression.
- Most of the trials ran 6-12 weeks, mostly in young men, and mostly on single-joint exercises.
