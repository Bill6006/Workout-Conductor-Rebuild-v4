# A lift to push (the owner's item 44)

Agreed text: "A lift to push: one lift and a target; it gets an extra weekly exposure and leads its
day while other work for those muscles holds (Ralston et al., 2017; Grgic et al., 2018), and
Progress shows the pace toward the target. The extra frequency and volume are checked against the
evidence first, so pushing one lift adds no needless fatigue or excess weekly volume."

**Status: built in Maintenance 27.** Every paper below was checked on PubMed (E-utilities) on
2026-10-10, its abstract read, and where a number needed more, its open full text. Two SportRxiv
preprints are marked as preprints wherever their numbers are used. The rules were then run through
a simulation of the app's own plan on eight schedules and five lifts (below), which changed three
of them, and an adversarial review of the built code, which changed eleven more (below).

## What it does

- **One lift and a target.** Settings, under Your training, "A lift to push": a main multi-joint
  lift with a heavy range and a load the app records (a bench press, a squat, a deadlift, a row, a
  machine press...), a target max, and an optional date. The push starts once both a lift and a
  target are set, and keeps its start while the target or the date changes.
- **One more session a week**, where the schedule has room: on a day whose plan has no place for
  the lift, two to three sets in the style's strength range, never to failure.
- **First in every session it is in**, and the time fit never leaves it out.
- **Its other work holds**: the coach adds no set, exercise or drop set to a lift for its muscles,
  and the extra never takes one of its muscles over twenty sets in any seven days it is in, nor
  further over than the day's plan without it.
- **Progress shows the pace** toward the target, with a band from the lifter's own scatter, once
  six weeks and ten sessions give it meaning; where it leads within twelve weeks; and a target date
  against it.
- **The coach** offers to end the push when the lift falls well short two sessions running, or when
  its pace is flat after six weeks, and a review every twelve weeks.
- **A deload week pauses it**: the deload applies to the lift as to the rest.

## The evidence

### More sessions of the lift

- More weekly sessions of a lift raise its 1RM, with diminishing returns: the largest step is from
  one to two a week, and no gain is detectable past about three (Pelland et al., 2026, PMID
  41343037; the preprint estimated +12.7% at one a week against +17.3% at two over an average study
  of 10.4 weeks, adjusted for volume).
- How much of that is apart from volume is contested: with volume equated, Grgic et al. (2018,
  PMID 29470825) and Ralston et al. (2018, PMID 30076500) found no significant effect, and every
  volume-equated trial in trained lifters was null on its own (Cuthbert et al., 2021, PMID
  33886099). Pelland 2026, the largest and newest analysis, found an effect after adjusting for
  volume and puts it down to practising the lift. Grgic 2018 is cited for "extra exposures add
  useful volume", Pelland 2026 for the exposure itself.
- The gain is specific to the exercise and equipment practised (Saeterbakken et al., 2025, PMID
  40314751), so the extra is the same lift.
- Frequency's effect on strength was in multi-joint lifts, not single-joint ones (Grgic 2018), so a
  push is on a main lift.
- Over 60, little gain beyond two sessions a week (Kneffel et al., 2021, PMID 32948100).

### Sets of the lift

- Strength gains for a lift flatten after a few weekly sets of it: 5-9 and 10 or more weekly sets
  both beat 5 or fewer (Ralston et al., 2017, PMID 28755103); 6 matched 9 (Enes et al., 2024, PMID
  38729165); little detectable gain past about 4-5 fractional sets (Pelland 2026).
- Spreading the same weekly sets over more sessions kept strength and size and lowered the effort
  of each (Johnsen and van den Tillaar, 2021, PMID 33643706; Ochi et al., 2018, PMID 30013480; Li
  et al., 2025, PMID 41210082), and muscle growth does not depend on frequency at equal volume
  (Schoenfeld, Grgic and Krieger, 2019, PMID 30558493).
- So the lift aims at six to nine weekly sets, never over ten; with fewer than six the extra adds
  sets, with six or more most of its sets come from the lift's other days.

### Going first

- Multi-joint strength gained more when multi-joint lifts came first (Nunes et al., 2021, PMID
  32077380: ES 0.32); strength gains are largest in the exercises done first (Simão et al., 2012,
  PMID 22292516; ACSM 2026, PMID 41843416). The lift it moves from first place may gain a little
  less: that is the trade "holds" accepts.

### The extra session

- Heavy: higher loads build the most 1RM (ACSM 2026; Currier et al., 2023, PMID 37414459).
- Short: two to three sets a session (ACSM 2026; a per-session plateau near two direct sets,
  Remmert et al., 2025, preprint).
- Short of failure: strength gains were similar across a wide range of reserve (Robinson et al.,
  2024, PMID 38970765), and failure slows recovery by a day or two (Morán-Navarro et al., 2017, PMID
  28965198). Daily maximal work for two weeks cut a squat 1RM by 12 kg (Fry et al., 1994, PMID
  7808252).

### Spacing

- Heavy work short of failure recovers within about 24-48 hours; sets to failure take 48 hours or
  more (Morán-Navarro 2017); after 100% of a 3RM, contractility took 33 hours to come back
  (Raastad and Hallén, 2000, PMID 10929214); high-volume sessions take 72 hours or more (Bartolomei
  et al., 2017, PMID 28447186).
- A heavy day did better when it did not directly follow the high-volume day (Zourdos et al., 2016,
  PMID 26332783).

### Its other work holds

- Keeping size and strength needs far less than building them: a third and even a ninth of the
  dose kept young adults' gains for 32 weeks (Bickel, Cross and Bamman, 2011, PMID 21131862); one
  session and one set per exercise a week, at the same relative load (Spiering et al., 2021, PMID
  33629972).
- Hypertrophy keeps rising with weekly sets, with diminishing returns; 12-20 sets a week are
  recommended for trained men, and the evidence for more turns mixed (Schoenfeld, Ogborn and
  Krieger, 2017, PMID 27433992; Baz-Valle et al., 2022, PMID 35291645).

### Pace

- A logged max carries about 5% noise per reading: the 1RM's day-to-day variation is 4.2% (Grgic
  et al., 2020, PMID 32681399), reps to failure vary 0.7 to 1.1 reps on retest (Mitter et al., 2022,
  PMID 35511896), and reserve is misjudged by about a rep (Halperin et al., 2022, PMID 34542869).
- So two single readings cannot show a change under about 12% (Weir, 2005, PMID 15705040), and a
  trend needs weeks: at two sessions a week, about 8 weeks to resolve ±0.9% a week and 12 to resolve
  ±0.5% (computed; the research file's table).
- Gains slow with time and strength, on a linear-log curve (Steele et al., 2023, PMID 35591809;
  Latella et al., 2020, PMID 32865942, and 2022, PMID 35019902; Solberg et al., 2019, PMID
  30958059), and short trials overstate the long-run pace (Hamarsland et al., 2022, PMID
  35069251), so a projection stays within twelve weeks.
- A fall in performance that lasts is the one marker of overreaching the evidence trusts (Grandou
  et al., 2020, PMID 31820373).

## The rules as built

`src/engine/planning/pushLift.ts`, `pushPace.ts`, the generator, the coach.

1. **Which lifts.** Compound, a strength range in the catalog, strength suitability 2 or more, and a
   load the app records (not bodyweight, not a band). 25 catalog lifts.
2. **Its own place.** Where today's plan has a place for the lift's movement, the lift takes it and
   leads. It gives the place up (another lift takes it) the day after its last session, or once it
   has three sessions in the last seven days (two from 60). Days are the device's calendar days, a
   workout counted on the day it began.
3. **The extra session.** On a day with no place for it, when all of these hold:
   - the plan's own sessions of it, over the last fourteen days, come to two a week or fewer (one
     from 60): one more while it has fewer than three;
   - fewer than three sessions of it (two from 60) in the last seven days, its family's lifts
     counted half;
   - none this calendar week yet;
   - two days since its last session (48 hours), three after one of five or more sets or a set at
     no reserve (72), on the calendar, so Monday, Wednesday and Friday at the same hour are two days
     apart whatever the clock says;
   - its week has room for two sets under ten;
   - **the next session does not have its own place for it**, when that session comes within two
     days (the next day of a fixed schedule, or with more days open than sessions, the week shared
     out evenly); the app looks ahead with its own template choice, the extra included;
   - **it takes none of its muscles over twenty sets in any seven days it is in**, nor further over
     than the day's plan without it: the days before as logged, and the sessions the schedule has in
     the six days after as the plan would build them, each counted as planned, drop sets included;
     sets of an isolation lift for that muscle come off today first (down to one each), then its own
     sets come down to two. On its day no drop set goes on a lift for its muscles: the extra is their
     added volume.
     It is the style's heavy range (an undulating plan's heavy day) at the style's reserve, never
     less than one: three sets, two once the week holds six, two under Lean-down. Under Light weights
     it is the style's own longer range, and under Foundation the style's moderate one, which keeps a
     new lifter off the heavy range: there its line says "short of failure", not "heavy". Where none
     goes in, the plan's Why says why ("No extra Barbell Bench Press today: the next session has its
     own place for it."); where the lift cannot go in today (no barbell at the place, or swapped out
     for weeks) none is weighed. A rebuild of a day with its extra in it (under way, swapped, or
     stopped and stood in for) keeps it, weighs no second one, and holds its cap and its drop-set
     rule; a lift kept from before keeps its sets, and the cap's line names only lifts still in the
     plan. The days ahead are calendar days on the device, so a clock change skips none.
4. **Sets.** In a week with the extra, or with three sessions, each session of the lift takes at
   most three sets (two under Lean-down); any week holds no more than ten.
5. **Never to failure**: one rep in reserve at least, in every session of the push. "Make it
   harder" takes a rep of reserve off it no lower than one and adds no set to it, and the time fit
   takes back the sets harder added to other lifts, never one of the extra's. "Make it easier"
   takes a set off it as off a fresh extra, never under two. One rule serves a fresh plan and a
   lift kept from before, and a lift whose push ended during the session keeps its sets.
6. **Leads**: first in every session it is in, kept through rebuilds; the day's main lift stays the
   main lift, second, ahead of accessories for muscles behind their week. The time fit treats both as
   main lifts. The day's extra swapped for another exercise (for comfort or pain too, and that swap
   is kept as a replacement is), or stopped and stood in for, keeps its place first and is never the
   day's main lift (the swap is not the lift to push); the plan names it first. The lift swapped away
   stays away that day, and a lasting swap never brings it back on a day it gives its place up.
   Where the lift cannot go in (no barbell at the place, or swapped out for weeks), the plan keeps
   its own order and names what leads.
7. **Holds**: while the push runs, the coach makes no offer that adds a set, an exercise, a drop set
   or more sets at fewer reps to a lift whose primary muscles share one with the pushed lift.
8. **Deload**: pauses the push.
9. **Pace**: each session's first working set since the push began, up to 12 reps with 3 or fewer
   in reserve, read as weight × (1 + (reps + reserve) / 30); a least-squares line through their
   logarithms, each rep range at its own level; the reading furthest off is set aside when it lies
   more than three spreads from the line fitted without it (a reading inside the spread it is judged
   by can hide itself), the spread never taken under a logged max's own noise of about 5% (Grgic et
   al. 2020), so a lift logged the same every session does not make its first real step look like a
   slip. Rounding in the sums reads as no slope and no spread: such a stall reads flat. Progress and
   the review say what a reading is, and how many sessions since the push began gave none. Shown
   after six weeks and ten readings, once its 95% band is narrower than the pace or twelve weeks
   have passed; projected only within twelve weeks; a date is "unlikely" when the needed pace is
   past the band's top, and "ambitious" past 1.5% a week (2% for a new lifter), what trained and
   new lifters gained in study blocks (Abe et al., 2000, PMID 10638374; Johnsen 2021; Refsnes et
   al., 2025, PMID 40580213); group figures, never a limit.
10. **Coach**: "End the push" when the lift's first working set fell three reps or more short of
    what it asked in each of its last two sessions since the push began (the hard start's reading),
    or when the pace's band tops out at or under zero after six weeks; a review each twelve weeks,
    which says what a pace still needs when there is none. Each has its own Not now, a run of falling
    short named by its latest session. Ending the push takes away its extra session; the lift keeps
    its own place.

### What the simulation changed

`src/engine/planning/pushLift.simulation.test.ts` runs the app's own generator session by session
for ten weeks on eight fixed schedules (2 to 6 days; build muscle and strength) with each of five
lifts pushed (bench press, back squat, deadlift, overhead press, barbell row), every session logged
as planned and an hour long, beside the same schedule with no push. The ordinary suite asserts in
every run: never more than three sessions or ten sets of the lift in any seven days; first in every
session; never under one in reserve; its sessions on different days, an extra two days after the
last (three after five sets); one extra a calendar week; an extra never the day before a session
with the lift's own place; no seven days with an extra in them past twenty sets for any of the
lift's muscles, or further past than that day's plan without the extra; and never meaningfully
fewer sessions of the lift than without the push. With `SIMULATE=1` it prints the tables.

Three rules from the research's proposal did not survive it:

- **The cap of "the weekly target plus three".** The plan already runs glutes and triceps above it
  (17.7 glute sets a week on three days; 24 triceps sets on four, with the arms a priority), so it
  blocked nearly every squat, bench and press extra. The cap is the evidence's own bound: twenty
  sets a week (Baz-Valle 2022), and the extra never takes a muscle past it.
- **A rolling seven days for "an extra a week".** On two days a week it let the extra in only every
  other week; it is the calendar week.
- **"Add one while the count is below three" on the last seven days.** On six days a week the squat
  already came about three times a week; an extra in a week that had two crowded out the next one
  of its own. The count is the plan's own sessions over fourteen days.

And one rule was added: the look-ahead. On Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday the bench press's
places come Monday and Friday; an extra on Thursday made Friday's place give way to a variation,
leaving the lift fewer sets than without the push. Such a week fits only two sessions two days
apart, and the plan already gives two, so the extra waits.

| Schedule          | Lift           | Sessions a week, before → after     | Sets a week |
| ----------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------- |
| 3 days (M W F)    | Bench press    | 2.1 → 3.1                           | 7.3 → 8.3   |
| 3 days            | Back squat     | 2.1 → 3.1                           | 8.3 → 8.3   |
| 3 days            | Deadlift       | 0 → 3.1                             | 0 → 8.3     |
| 3 days            | Overhead press | 0 (a variation 1.0) → 2.1           | 0 → 6.3     |
| 4 days (M T Th F) | Bench press    | 2.1 → 2.1 (no room two days apart)  | 8.3 → 8.3   |
| 4 days            | Overhead press | 0 (a variation 1.0) → 2.1           | 0 → 5.2     |
| 4 days (M W F Sa) | Bench press    | 1.5 → 2.0 (its triceps near twenty) | 5.8 → 6.4   |
| 2 days (M Th)     | Bench press    | 1.4 → 2.1                           | 4.9 → 7.2   |
| 6 days, strength  | Back squat     | 2.8 → 2.8                           | 11.1 → 8.3  |

(Weeks 3 to 10, re-run after the review; "before" is the same plan with no push. The deadlift and
overhead press have no "before" because the plan picks a dumbbell Romanian deadlift and a dumbbell
shoulder press for those places.)

### What the review changed

An adversarial review of the built code found eleven faults; each fix is tested, and each test
fails with its fix reverted.

- **Hours became calendar days.** The plan is made at the start of a session and the last one was
  read at its end, so Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 17:00, an hour each, read 47 hours apart and
  the extra never came (the simulation had logged every session as ending when it began). Spacing,
  the own place's day-after rule and the seven-day counts now read the device's calendar days.
- **The cap reads the days after the extra too.** It read only the days before, and later sessions
  carried rolling weeks with an extra to 21-22 sets of a pushed lift's muscle. It now plans the
  sessions ahead; the drop set it could not count goes off the extra's day.
- **A harder day** had rebuilt the pushed lift from its role's own prescription: five sets, or none
  in reserve under Light weights. It now keeps to the push's sets and one in reserve.
- **A swapped extra** read as missing on the next rebuild: the lift came back as a second extra
  and the swap went last.
- **The main lift** fell behind accessories for muscles behind their week when the pushed lift led.
- **The lift's slot** moved first even when the lift could not go in, so its stand-in led and the
  lines named the wrong lift.
- **End the push** read falls from before the push began, and two Not nows on it silenced the flat
  pace's offer too; the review read "the readings scatter" when there were simply too few.
- **The pace's outlier** was judged against a spread that included it, so a slip could hide itself
  where the pace first shows.
- **The probe** counted a kept extra twice on a rebuild.
- **Two days a week** went back to full body, upper and lower: full body alone left calves, abs,
  side delts and upper chest untrained (`docs/research/real-week.md`).

### What the re-check changed

A second adversarial pass over the review's fixes, run against the code with probes, found ten
more; each fix is tested, and each test fails with its fix reverted.

- **The day's extra through every change.** Swapped before it began, it was named the day's main
  lift, so the squat lost its protected place at 30 and 15 minutes; swapped for comfort or pain, it
  was lost on the next rebuild; begun and then swapped, it went to the end of the plan and was
  weighed again ("No extra ... at the weekly cap" on the day it was begun). The day's extra now
  leads whatever it holds, a swap of it is kept, its stand-in carries it on, and a rebuild weighs
  no second one.
- **The cap and the drop-set rule through a rebuild.** Rebuilt with the extra under way, the day
  gave back the sets the cap had taken (triceps to 21 in seven days) and could plan a drop set on
  the pushed lift's muscles. Both now hold through every rebuild.
- **Harder and easier.** "Make it harder" could trim one of the extra's own sets to fit the time
  while the squat took an added one; "Make it easier" left the extra in front at three sets where a
  fresh one had two.
- **What the plan says.** The cap's line named a lift the time fit had left out; Foundation's
  extra (6 to 10 reps, 3 in reserve) was called heavy; a swapped extra's day named the main lift
  first.
- **Where the lift cannot go.** At home without a barbell, or swapped out for weeks, the plan still
  weighed the extra, eight plans' work, and could say the triceps were at their cap.
- **A lasting swap into the pushed lift** put it back the day after a session of it.
- **A clock change.** The next session and the days ahead stepped 24 hours, so a plan made at
  23:30 the night the clocks went forward skipped Sunday; they step calendar days now.
- **A stall logged the same every session** never read as flat (its band topped out at 2 × 10⁻¹⁶,
  not 0), and after it a real step up of five pounds was set aside as a slip.
- **No reading under Light weights.** The pushed lift is logged at 10 to 15 reps there, past the 12
  a reading takes, and the cards said only that the first session would give one.
- **The probe** weighed the day with a drop set the extra's day would not plan, and looked ahead
  without the coach's focus; neither changed a plan in the probes, and both now match the real day.

The tests that ran this behaviour also hold in any time zone now (the simulation began at 17:00
UTC, which is the next day east of UTC+7).

### Not built (beyond the agreed text)

From the research's proposals, left for the owner to ask for if wanted:

- the coach suggesting a push to a lifter past the beginner stage;
- on the extra's day, replacing a same-muscle exercise's sets with the lift when its muscles are at
  their cap (the extra waits instead);
- a linear-log projection anchored on how long the lift has been trained;
- moving a Light weights lifter's pushed sets to the strength range: Settings says light loads build
  less max strength (Schoenfeld et al., 2017, PMID 28834797; Lopez et al., 2021, PMID 33433148)
  and the style stands.

## Uncertainty

- Frequency apart from volume in trained lifters: every volume-equated trial in trained lifters was
  null on its own; for them, most of what a push buys may be its sets, its place first and its heavy
  sets.
- No trial has tested "push one lift, hold the rest"; the rules are assembled from frequency,
  volume, order, maintenance and recovery evidence.
- Spacing rests on acute recovery studies of 10-17 people measuring proxies.
- The pace's band is modelled from test-retest and reserve studies; the noise of a max read from an
  app's logged sets has not been measured directly. Readings logged in other units mix until the
  units change back.
- The cost to the lift that loses first place is unknown.
