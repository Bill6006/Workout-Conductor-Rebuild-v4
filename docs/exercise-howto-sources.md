# Exercise How to: sources

Maintenance 25, item 7. Each exercise's How to (Setup, Do it, Key cues, Avoid, and How far
where it matters) is written for someone who has never done the exercise: plain words, two to
four steps, one to three cues and one to three things to avoid. Every line was checked against
the sources listed under it; a study is cited where it settles a choice (how deep, how wide,
which variation). Each text matches the exercise's demonstration (docs/media-license-register.md);
where the demonstration shows a different setup from the first draft, the text was changed to
it, and the change is noted under the exercise. Where a demonstration differs in a detail the text
should keep (the ninth review found eight: a narrower grip, a deeper leg press, handles at the
shoulders, a forward lean, heels on a bench, an arm a little high, straight arms, a deeper
pullover), a note under the demonstration says so and the text holds. "Spotter" is never said:
the steps ask for a helper behind you (the ninth and tenth reviews; a test holds it).

Sources marked secondary are coaching references used beside a primary one. A book marked
"print; not opened" is cited for what it is widely known to say, never alone.

## Barbell Bench Press (`barbell-bench-press`)

- Haff GG, Triplett NT (eds), 2016, Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning, 4th ed., ch. 15 Exercise Technique for Free Weight and Machine Training (print; not opened). Supports: setup 1-3 (five-point body contact, eyes below the bar, grip slightly wider than shoulders); steps 1-3 (bar lowered to touch the chest, pressed to straight arms); cue 3 (a spotter for lifts done over the face).
- ACE Exercise Library, n.d., Chest Press (barbell, flat bench). https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/5/chest-press/ Supports: setup 1-3 (grip slightly wider than shoulders, feet pressed into the floor, hips on the bench, lift off the rack); steps 1 and 3.
- Noteboom L et al., 2024, Effects of bench press technique variations on musculoskeletal shoulder loads and potential injury risk, Front Physiol. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38974522/ Supports: setup 2 and cue 2 (a grip under 1.5 shoulder widths and shoulder blades squeezed back lowered shoulder-joint loads); cue 1 and avoid 1 (full text PMC11224528: elbows at 90 degrees raised glenohumeral compression, 45 degrees raised one shear force, and elbow angle mattered less than grip width and shoulder-blade position).
- ACSM, n.d., Selecting and Effectively Using Free Weights (consumer brochure; official acsm.org link now returns 404, copy opened here). https://www.prescriptiontogetactive.com/static/pdfs/selecting-and-effectively-using-free-weights.pdf Supports: cue 3 (find a partner to spot you when lifting heavy weights).

## Incline Barbell Bench Press (`incline-barbell-bench-press`)

- Rodriguez-Ridao D et al., 2020, Effect of five bench inclinations on the electromyographic activity of the pectoralis major, anterior deltoid, and triceps brachii during the bench press exercise, Int J Environ Res Public Health. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33049982/ Supports: setup 1 and avoid 1 (upper chest most active at 30 degrees; above 45 degrees the front shoulder works more and the chest less).
- Lauver JD et al., 2016, Influence of bench angle on upper extremity muscular activation during bench press exercise, Eur J Sport Sci. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25799093/ Supports: setup 1 (30 and 45 degrees both raised upper-chest activity over flat during part of the press).
- Trebs AA et al., 2010, An electromyography analysis of 3 muscles surrounding the shoulder joint during the performance of a chest press exercise at several angles, J Strength Cond Res. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20512064/ Supports: notes only (upper chest was more active at 44 than at 28 degrees, so the best angle is not settled).
- ACE Exercise Library, n.d., Incline Chest Press (dumbbells). https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/25/incline-chest-press/ Supports: steps 1-2 (lower toward the upper chest and touch gently without bouncing); cue 2 (head, shoulders, hips and feet stay in contact; no low-back arch).
- Haff GG, Triplett NT (eds), 2016, Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning, 4th ed., ch. 15 (print; not opened). Supports: cue 3 (a spotter for free-weight lifts done over the face).

## Dip (`dip`)

- McKenzie A et al., 2022, Bench, bar, and ring dips: do kinematics and muscle activity differ?, Int J Environ Res Public Health. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36293792/ Supports: range and cue 2 (full text PMC9603242: bar dips used about 88% of each lifter's maximum shoulder extension; the deepest point is the dip's most vulnerable position for the front of the shoulder; body-weight bar dips may overload shoulder structures in less experienced lifters); setup 2 (leaning forward shares the depth between trunk lean and shoulder extension).
- Bernstorff MA et al., 2023, Popular gym fitness sport: an analysis of 1387 recreational athletes regarding prone to pain exercises and the corresponding localisations, Sports (Basel). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38251286/ Supports: range and cue 2 (dips were among the exercises most often linked to pain, and the shoulder was the most common pain site).

## Cable Crunch (`cable-crunch`)

- ACE, n.d., Exercise Library: Standing Crunch (cable, rope). https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/331/standing-crunch/ Supports: setup 1 (rope on the highest pulley); setup 2 (rope ends held by the ears); step 1 (roll the rib cage down against the cable); step 2 (return slowly); cue 2.
- ACE, n.d., Exercise Library: Crunch. https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/52/crunch/ Supports: step 1 and cue 1 (bring the rib cage toward the pelvis); avoid 2 (letting the hip flexors help tilts the pelvis and stresses the lower back).

## Pallof Press (`pallof-press`)

- ACE, n.d., Exercise Library: Standing Anti-rotation Press. https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/332/standing-anti-rotation-press/ Supports: setup 1 (pulley at chest height, directly to the side); setup 2 (one handle in both hands, elbows in); setup 3 (knees slightly bent); steps 1-3 (press forward, hold 2-3 seconds, return slowly); cue 2 and avoid 1 (back straight and tall, no turning).
