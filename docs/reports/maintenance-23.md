# Maintenance 23: round E, bodyweight lifts and effort

Built on 2026-09-24 on the owner's word: "Proposed approved. GREEN - NEXT PHASE" (Maintenance 22).
Round E is the owner's items 2, 5, 21, 22, 24, 25, 26 and 27: drop sets and failure only where
they belong, a truthful drop-set switch, light sets that still reach the planned effort, warm-ups
and breaks on bodyweight lifts, and three wording fixes. Maintenance 21 stays YELLOW until the

## 2. No drop sets or failure where they do not belong

**Found:** the owner's lower day gave the Dead Bug a drop set, with no weight to drop, and a
target of no reps in reserve; failure on a Dead Bug is the lower back lifting off the floor.

**Delivered:**

- No drop set on a lift with no weight to take off (bodyweight, a band, and Bench Dip and
  Step-Up, which list only a bench) or on a core stability move (the anti-extension and
  anti-rotation moves: Plank, Dead Bug, Ab Wheel Rollout and Pallof Press).
  The rule sits in the exercise list itself, so the plan, the coach's offer and a drop set added
  by hand all follow it ("Dead Bug is not safe for a drop set."). An exercise the lifter made
  follows it too, whatever its drop-set switch says.
- A core stability move keeps two reps in reserve under every style and role, and "Make it
  harder" never takes it under two. Its effort row reads "stop 2 clean reps short, before your
  form slips". Other moves keep their failure where the style allows it.
- A plan saved before the update loses a drop set the exercise no longer suits when it is next
  refreshed, and its "Drop set" badge goes with it.

## 21. Light sets still reach the planned effort

**Found:** when the weights at a place cannot make a target (mostly light dumbbells at home), the
app took the nearest weight under it and added at most three reps. Well short of the target, that
left a muscle-building set with far more in reserve than planned: an easy set.

**Delivered:**

- A muscle-building set more than 10% lighter than asked runs to its planned reps in reserve. The
  reps follow the effort (the same estimated-max match as before, no longer stopped at three, and
  never past 30), and the line says so: "The weights here make 40, not 50 lb: about 10 more reps,
  to 2 in reserve." At the heaviest weight a place has: "Held at the heaviest weight here (40 lb):
  about 10 more reps, to 2 in reserve." At no reps in reserve it reads "to the last clean rep".
- A strength set keeps the old rule, one to three extra reps, and so does a set 10% light or less.
- Where reaching the reserve would take more than 30 reps, the set stops at 30 and the line
  promises no reserve: "about 20 more reps, up to 30".
- Such a set keeps its own tempo (the slower tempo at the heaviest weight is for strength sets):
  the reps bring the effort. It takes no drop set, from the plan or the coach, and the coach's
  "at the heaviest weight here" card offers "Add a set" only once its reps reach 30.
- The set remembers the load and range it stood in for, and the next session counts it toward
  them. Shown 25-29 at 20 lb for a 30 lb target of 6-10, a set that met 25-29 met the target: the
  next target stays at 30 lb and is fitted to the weights again (without the record it fell to
  20 lb for 6-10), and a lift trained at home and at the gym keeps one target. The reps are judged
  as logged, against the reps shown, so 25 to 28 reps at the same light weight reads as progress.
  The "Last:" line still shows what was lifted, and a second line says what it stood in for: "At
  20 lb, those reps stood in for 30 lb."
- A push that stops short of the effort (a strength set, a small push, or 30 reps at most) holds
  the load asked: "The weights last time made less than 35 lb: the same target again." Missing
  the reps shown is a miss, as anywhere, and a deload then comes down from the weight lifted.
- Sessions at light weights are left out of stall calls and the history notes, whose advice would
  move a load the place cannot make. The finish summary compares a lift by the fuller of its two
  estimates, so 30 lb × 8 at the gym is not "progressed" over 20 lb × 29 at home.
- The same rule holds when the plan is made, after a swap, when the weights change before or
  during an exercise (more weights give the load back), when the place changes mid-lift, and
  after a max is entered. Reps set by hand stay when the weights change, and "the next sets go
  up", or a weight the coach sets, gives a pushed set the range asked once it reaches the load
  asked.

The research is in `docs/research/lighter-loads.md`, both papers checked on PubMed: light and
heavy loads build muscle about equally when sets are hard enough (Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn and
Krieger, 2017, PMID 28834797), and closeness to failure matters for muscle much more than for
strength (Robinson et al., 2024, PMID 38970765). The 10% line is a judgement; no study set it.

## 22. Warm-ups on bodyweight lifts

**Found:** a chin-up got one or two warm-up sets asking for its whole working range ("Target
6-12", "Easy, RIR 5"): at bodyweight nothing comes off, so the warm-up was a hard set.

**Delivered,** as `docs/research/bodyweight-warm-ups.md` sets out:

- At most one warm-up set on a lift done at bodyweight, of a few easy reps: about a third to a
  half of the bottom of the working range (2-3 before sets of 6-12, 1 before 3-6), never more
  than the working sets ask.
- Its hints say it plainly: "A few easy reps" and "Stop well short".
- The same rule holds for the plan, every rebuild (a swap, a change of weights, a max), "+ Ramp
  set" and a change to the rep range. "+ Ramp set" is not offered while the lift has its warm-up;
  one skipped with "Skip warm-up sets" was never done, so one can be added back.
- Back after twenty minutes away, the lift in front warms up again with a few easy reps and no
  weight; it used to ask for the whole range at 5 lb. A logged warm-up opened to correct it
  keeps the plain hints.

## 24. Coming back after a break on bodyweight lifts

**Found:** a weighted lift back after three weeks or more starts lighter, but a lift done at
bodyweight has no estimated max, so it came back with the same target as before; and the "short
of 6 reps twice in a row" card could fire from sessions weeks old.

**Delivered:**

- After 21 days or more, the first session back starts at the bottom of the range with a rep more
  in reserve (two after 42 days): "25 days since the last session: start at the bottom of the
  range, 6-8 reps, with a rep more in reserve." The line names only the reserve it adds (reserve
  tops out at 4) and says "start at 4-6 reps" where the range is already that short.
- The session after that reads the first one back as part of today's range: 8 reps there is
  inside 6-12, not "the top of the range".
- Misses from before a break of three weeks or more stop counting, so the fewer-reps card waits
  for fresh sessions. The same holds for a loaded lift: a miss from before the break no longer
  counts towards taking 10% off. A break means no session of the lift at any rep range, so a
  lift trained once a week in rotating ranges still counts its misses.
- The history notes compare only the sessions since the last break, so a chin-up back at 6-8 is
  not "reps have flattened" against the 10s from before it.

## Known limits

- **Short sessions at light dumbbells.** Sets run to their effort take longer (25-30 reps), so at
  home with dumbbells up to 20 lb a 30 or 45 minute session can leave out one exercise to fit:
  Incline Dumbbell Press on the push day at 30 minutes, Chest-Supported Row and Dumbbell Romanian
  Deadlift on the full-body day at 30 and 45. On the pull day at 30 minutes it leaves out
  Chin-Up while two 25-30 rep shrug sets stay, because on short sessions the time fit keeps paired
  moves first. Default-length sessions keep every exercise and run up to 5 minutes longer.
  Agreed 33 changes which exercise gives way: the main lifts stay and an isolation exercise goes
  first (`docs/research/short-sessions.md`).
- A plan saved before this update keeps a Dead Bug drop set that was already planned until it is
  refreshed or planned again; a new day's plan never has one. In such a plan, a lift the step rule
  held is pushed if the weights change mid-lift.
- Back after twenty minutes away, a bodyweight lift that had its warm-up gets one more easy set,
  as a loaded lift gets a light ramp.
- A deload week at a place whose weights make less than asked lowers the load asked there for
  the sessions after it: back at home, a strength lift keeps standing in for 55 lb where 60 lb was
  asked, since the load asked holds while the weights cannot make it. It returns once the lift is
  trained where they do; at the gym, the next session.
- Unchanged from before: a change of place or length rebuilds the rest of the workout without
  "Make it harder" (Agreed 34, `docs/research/effort-setting.md`); and the "Last:" line shows a
  session's heaviest weight beside
  every set's reps, so a lift whose weight moved mid-session reads as if every set was at it.
- Unchanged from before, found by the fourth review: a miss at the gym and then a short miss at
  home deloads from the home weight (60 lb to 45 lb, 25% at the gym, not 10%); the fatigue read
  counts a light home session after a gym one as a fall in estimated max; and where the
  dumbbells jump (30, then 40), "the reps go up first" comes back each session, so the load that
  line says will follow never does there.
- A ramp set added by hand to a lift not yet begun goes when the place or the weights change: the
  lift's ramps are planned again for its new weight (as they already were for a change of
  weights).
- A ramp still to come that a lower working weight took away (a change of weights or of place)
  does not come back when the working weight does: the lift goes on with the ramps it has, and
  "+ Ramp set" adds one.
- Reps set by hand on a set the weights had already pushed (20 lb × 12-15 standing in for 30 lb ×
  6-10) stay at the lower weight when the weights dip further (15 lb) and come back: the record
  keeps the load the set stands in for, not the weight the reps were set at, and remembering that
  would take a new stored field.
- A max entered for a lift whose weight is set for today leaves that lift as it is and is saved;
  once the lift is done that day, it counts for it after being entered again.
- Older than this round: at a place whose weights stop short of the first target, "Know your
  max?" names the target the lift will stand in for (100 lb × 6-10, from the max alone); the set
  then shows the heaviest weight there, with the reps that reach the effort.
- The coach's load offers ("Take 25 lb today", a deload) and rep-range routes, like its "add an
  exercise", do not look at the weights at the place or a deload week (older than this round;
  Agreed 35). A weight the coach sets now follows the push's rule.
- Unchanged from before: when reps are set by hand for the set in front, its reps dial keeps the
  number it showed until that set is logged or its card opens again; the target under the dial
  shows the new range at once.
- Unchanged from before: a loaded hold (a farmer carry) at a place whose weights stop short keeps
  no record of the load asked, so its next target comes from the weight held there, and a carry
  under way that moves to where the load is made stays at the lighter weight.
