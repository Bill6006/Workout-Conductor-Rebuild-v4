## 33. The main lifts stay when a short session runs over

**Found:** when a session at the length chosen could not fit every exercise, the time fit left out
the lowest-value row, and on short sessions paired rows counted extra. On the pull day at 30
minutes at home with dumbbells up to 20 lb it left out Chin-Up while two 25-30 rep shrug sets
stayed.

**Research:** when time is short, multi-joint exercises come first (Iversen et al. 2021, PMID
34125411); single-joint work added no arm size or strength on top of multi-joint work (Gentil et
al. 2013, PMID 23537028; de França et al. 2015, PMID 26244600; Gentil, Fisher and Steele 2017,
PMID 27677913), though it can matter for a muscle the main lifts barely train (Mannarino et al.
2021, PMID 31268995).

**Delivered:**

- The main lifts stay, and an isolation exercise gives way first. Among isolation exercises, one
  whose muscles the main lifts still in the plan train goes first (a curl on a day with rows and
  chin-ups); the day's only work for a muscle goes later, core work counting as that, and
  lower-back work last. A main lift gives way only once no isolation exercise is left.
- Down to two exercises, a row that still does not fit goes for a move left out that fits in its
  place, tried with its rest cut as far as the fit cuts rests (never longer than its own), then at
  fewer sets, down to two, and never under the sets the plan had before Make it harder added one;
  the line says so ("Kept Dumbbell Curl on its own at 2 sets"). This is how a main lift gives way
  (point 3 of the design): at 15 minutes at the gym an upper day keeps Barbell Bench Press and a
  Chin-Up in place of the barbell row, and at a home with dumbbells to 20 lb a push day keeps
  Dumbbell Bench Press and two sets of Dumbbell Curl in place of the incline press. With nothing
  that fits in its place, the row goes all the same, the main lift stays alone, and it takes back
  the sets the fit trimmed while the minutes fit them ("Gave Dumbbell Bench Press back a set"),
  since the time is then that lift's: a session shortened to 15 minutes with the bench press under
  way keeps the bench press alone, and a workout started at 15 minutes that moves home 4 minutes in
  keeps the dumbbell bench press alone at its 4 sets. With an exact end time the rows go one by
  one, and the lifts left take back their trimmed sets the same way, each where it sat: ending in 10
  minutes keeps the bench press alone at 3 sets, not 2. A circuit's count of rounds is the rounds it
  runs, its longest member's.
- When rows left out leave minutes unused, their moves come back on their own while they fit, the
  one this order keeps longest first. Rows the row cap left out come back too.
- The owner's case: the 30-minute pull day at home with dumbbells to 20 lb now keeps Chin-Up. The
  hammer curl goes first, then the curl and rear-delt pair, and the curl comes back on its own.

**Measured:** across 2,700 plans at 15, 30 and 45 minutes (the gym, home, and dumbbells to 15, 20
or 30 lb; five templates; four goals; default lengths of 45, 60 and 75 minutes; techniques on and
off), 1,128 changed: 666 keep more main lifts, and 48 keep one fewer, all at 15 minutes at the
light dumbbells, where the old order kept two main lifts that ran over: two presses, 2.4 or 2.6
minutes over, in 24, and a goblet squat with a dumbbell bench press on the full-body day, 1.6 over,
in the other 24. "Even the leanest version runs about N min over" shows on none of them now,
against 216 fifteen-minute plans with the old order. Across 480 rebuilds (sessions shortened while
not started, with the main lift pinned, under way, or one set in), none runs over where the old
order fit, and 69 of the 81 that ran over with it no longer do. A fresh plan at a default length
of 60 or 75 minutes changed in none of 600. The fixes of every re-check since change none of
these figures: both runs are the same, line for line.

## 34. The check-in, and Make it harder or easier, hold through every change

**A correction first:** the app has no "Make it harder" or "Make it easier" button. The engine has
had the setting since Phase 4 (the `intensity` trigger), but no screen ever sent it. My proposal
for this item, and Maintenance 23's phone step 3 ("also after Make it harder"), spoke of a button
that is not there; that step could not be done. The setting a lifter does see is the check-in,
and it had the same flaw.

**Found:** after a low check-in (low energy, sleep or motivation), the rest of the workout has a
set fewer on each exercise with one to spare and a rep more in reserve. Only the check-in applied
that: a change of length or place, coming back after a long break, or a change to the equipment,
the techniques or the profile rebuilt the rest at normal difficulty without saying so. The
engine's harder or easier setting was lost the same way.

**Research:** adjusting the day's effort to how the lifter feels, which is what the check-in
does, builds strength at least as well as a fixed plan (Zhang et al. 2021, PMID 33776802; Larsen,
Kristiansen and van den Tillaar 2021, PMID 33520457); and more sets and sets closer to failure
change what a session does (Pelland et al. 2026, PMID 41343037; Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger
2017, PMID 27433992; Robinson et al. 2024, PMID 38970765; Refalo et al. 2023, PMID 36334240), so
losing the adjustment quietly changes the session.

**Delivered:**

- Every rebuild of the rest of the workout keeps a low check-in's set fewer and extra rep in
  reserve: a new length, another place, coming back after a break, an exact end time, a change to
  the equipment, the techniques or the profile. The fit to time still comes last.
- A check-in back to fine brings back the planned sets and says "Planned sets and effort back".
  Before, it said "Feeling good: full workout kept." and left the cut sets, which the next change
  put back without a word. The words say what changed, both ways: "fewer sets", "an extra rep in
  reserve", or both; back to fine "Planned sets and effort back", or "Planned effort back" where the
  sets were at their fewest either way (15 and 30 minutes); and a sore check-in after a drained one
  "the planned effort back", the sets still cut. Sets are named only where the day's settings moved
  them, never for the fit to time, and a sore joint is named only where a lift it rules out (high
  stress on it, or a movement it rules out) left the plan or stopped for it. With nothing left to
  bring back (every lift done but the one under way, which keeps its sets), it says "Feeling good:
  nothing left to change."; a check-in that changes nothing says "Checked in: no changes needed."
- A check-in reaches every lift not begun that the plan keeps as it is, keeping its exercise: the
  lift in front until its first set is logged, one you pinned, and one you swapped in. It goes to
  the plan's own count under the new settings, never above the sets it has on a cut (the fit to
  time may have trimmed it) nor under them on a restore, keeps a ramp you added, and is then fitted
  to the time like any lift not begun, so at 15 minutes the bench press keeps its floor of 3 and
  nothing comes in on minutes a cut never freed. Before, those lifts kept their sets either way
  while the headline said the plan was back. A lift the coach added, one standing in for a stopped
  lift, one whose sets, reps or weight you set by hand, and one under way keep their sets.
- A plan not started yet that is built again from today's inputs (a coach focus set or cleared, a
  deload week that covers today planned or cancelled, a kept swap stopped) keeps today's choices:
  the length, the check-in, the plates missing today, and what was set aside for today (skips,
  sore joints, busy equipment, an end time still ahead). Before, it went back to a plain Default
  plan and the check-in card to "Quick check-in". A deload week that starts after today no longer
  builds today's plan again at all. A plan that cannot be built again stays as it was, the change
  goes with it (a coach focus, a deload week) and with the plan Undo brings back, and the overlay
  adds "The setting itself is saved."
- A plan still on the screen from an earlier day holds nothing chosen for that day. Built again,
  changed (a new length, a check-in), started, undone, given an exact end time, or saved around (a
  place renamed, new units), it starts from today's plan; a change to one of its exercises or
  pairings (a skip, a swap, a split), or an exercise added to it, has nothing left to change and
  shows today's plan. A saved workout loaded on it is today's plan and stays through the next
  change.
- A lift changed on its own keeps the extra rep in reserve it carries: its weights refitted (new
  weights, or a lift kept through a change of place), a max entered, a swap, a stand-in for a lift
  under way, and a lift picked up again. The effort is read on its first set still to come, and a
  reserve already at its limit (4) still carries the check-in to the exercise swapped in. It counts
  only as far as the day's settings reach, so a deload week's extra rep is never cancelled and a
  setting since taken back does not stay on.
- The engine's harder or easier setting holds the same way, with the check-in on top, within the
  reserve an exercise allows (a core stability move keeps its 2). It stays unreachable until a
  screen offers it; see Proposed.

## 35. The coach offers only what the place makes, and holds back in a deload week

**Found:** at a home whose dumbbells stop at 20 lb the coach could offer "Take 25 lb today"; its
micro-deload and a stalled lift's deload step could name a weight the place does not have (a
tenth off 20 lb rounded back to 20); during a deload week it could still offer more weight; and
the exercise it adds ignored the weights at the place.

**Delivered:**

- A weight the coach offers is the plan's own step, onto what the place makes today, missing
  plates counted. "Take N today" is one ordinary step up (5 lb for dumbbells and bars, 10 lb for a
  stack) from the weight last lifted, fitted onto the weights there, so 55 lb on a stack by 5 goes
  to 65, not 70. A micro-deload is the plan's, a tenth off and at least one step, from today's
  target or the weight last lifted, whichever is heavier: so 20 lb goes to 15, without the 2.5s
  135 lb goes to 115, and a bench press at 195 today (190 last) deloads to 175, not 170. A cut
  bigger than about a tenth says how big: "15 lb, 25% lighter, rebuilds the reps before adding
  load again."
- No step up is offered on a lighter day the plan chose, and its micro-deload card is left out:
  back after a break, at a new rep range, a deload or a reset, or a step down for the work before
  the lift today or your habit. The plan's own note says why it is lighter, and the plan now keeps
  those steps as data, so a weight the place only rounded down (52.5 typed where a gym's dumbbells
  go by 5, shown as 50) still gets its step, "Take 55 lb today". A plan made before this update,
  still on the screen the day it lands, is read by its own reasons. A weight set by hand under the
  one last lifted, the coach's own deload among them, gets no step up and no second deload.
- A set the weights here push (it shows less than the plan asks, with more reps) is offered no
  step up, and its micro-deload or deload comes down from the weight it shows: where the dumbbells
  stop at 45 and the plan asks 50, "Deload to 40 lb this week".
- A card whose step the place does not make is left out, the lift under way or not (the heaviest
  weight there, dumbbells that jump from 50 to 60, a plate missing). Before, it came back once the
  first set was logged ("ready for more load", "take the next load step now").
- A card whose step today's target already has is left out: a step the plan or the session took,
  or a target a tenth or more down already (its deload, a reset, a return after a break). That
  holds for "ready for more load", sets ending too easy, a stalling lift, and a stalled lift's
  deload step, which is then left out that day rather than passed over. Before, the coach stepped
  from today's target, so it offered a second step on top of the plan's: "Take 195 lb" over the
  plan's 190, or "Micro-deload to 40 lb" under the plan's 45.
- A route step that changes the plan is in place while the change runs, so its card never offers
  it again once the overlay goes, is saved once the change lands, and is taken back when the change
  fails, so a failed change passes no step over. A step that cannot be saved stays for now, and the
  app says "The change is made, but this step could not be saved: the coach may offer it again."
- Where the place moves the step onto a nearer weight, the reason says that weight ("Next step:
  52.5 lb, the nearest weight here").
- A stalled lift's route step the weights here cannot give is passed over, and the card offers
  the next step: a new rep range on sets the dumbbells hold well short of their load (6-10 reps at
  20 lb would sit far from the effort), or a deload where nothing lighter is made. The route line
  marks it "(passed over)" while the place cannot give it, taking the next step moves the route on
  for good, and a route done with steps passed over says so rather than that every step was tried.
  A set only a little short of its load shifts from the range it stands in for.
- In a deload week the coach says nothing that pushes for more: no plateau card, no weekly-coverage
  card, nothing at the heaviest weight here, and no offer of an extra set, a new rep range or a drop
  set. The deload week's lighter loads and extra rep in reserve are the answer to a stalled lift and
  a muscle behind its week. Safety, saving, recovery, the note on a lowered load, a longer rest
  while the workout is on, and the goals style card still speak. A light lift whose load a tenth off
  rounds back to itself no longer says "loads 10% lighter", nor does one the heaviest weight here
  holds either way (20 lb dumbbells), and "Why this workout" names lighter loads only when a lift
  has them (a lift under way keeps its line through new weights: known limits). At the heaviest
  weight the deload still shows: a rep more in reserve, and a lighter load stood in for, so fewer
  extra reps.
- The exercise the coach adds is fitted as the plan fits one: to the weights at the place (at the
  light home, a dumbbell row at 20 lb standing in for its load), today's fatigue and the day's
  settings.

## Found while building

- **A deload week cancelled by the carried effort.** The first version of 34 read a lift's
  effort against a plain target that already held the deload week's extra rep. A lift planned
  before the week read as "made harder" and a max or a change of weights took the deload off it.
  The carried effort now counts only as far as the day's settings reach.
- **The refill ignored the new order.** In the first version of 33, a 30-minute upper day at home
  brought back a curl on its own while the lateral raise, the day's only shoulder work, stayed out.
  The refill now tries first what the order keeps longest.
- **A deload week still let some offers through.** The first version held back load offers,
  stalls and added exercises, but still offered "Add the set", "Add a set" at the heaviest weight,
  "Aim one rep higher", fewer reps over more sets, and a drop set; and the coverage card, with its
  exercise held back, said "No room today" when there was room. One rule now holds back
  everything that pushes for more.
- **No button for Make it harder.** Writing the browser check for 34 showed that no screen sends
  the setting (the correction under 34).

## Known limits

- **A 45-minute default is a 45-minute session.** For a lifter whose usual length is 45 minutes,
  Default follows the new order like choosing 45 (40 of the 300 such plans checked changed).
- **Rebuilds that still run over.** 12 of the 480 rebuilds checked run more than a minute over, as
  the old order did: a goblet squat under way at light dumbbells that alone needs 1.2 to 1.9
  minutes more than the minutes left (the old order ran up to 10.6 over, with a second exercise).
- **At a gap in the dumbbells, the load does not move on** (older than this round): with 50s and
  then 60s, the plan holds 50 and raises the reps, and the coach offers no jump either. Proposed.
- **A deload week can leave a light load as it was** (older than this round): a tenth off 25 lb
  rounds back to 25. It no longer says it is lighter. Proposed.
- **A check-in taken back while a lift is under way.** That lift keeps its set fewer and extra
  rep; swapped later, the exercise swapped in takes the plain reserve but keeps the one set fewer.
- **A check-in's rep at the reserve limit.** A lift at 4 in reserve (back after a long break) that
  was under way before a low check-in passes the check-in's rep to an exercise swapped in for it.
- **A plan built again keeps today's choices, not its rows.** A pin, a swap for today, an exercise
  the coach added, or sets and reps set by hand belonged to rows of the old plan, so a coach focus
  set before the workout starts rebuilds without them; a coach card taken or set aside today can
  come back (older than this round).
- **The app's day ends at midnight UTC** (older than this round): 8 pm in New York in summer, 7 pm
  in winter. A plan not started yet goes back to a fresh plan then, with no word; this round makes
  every path agree with that day, including the Plan tab. Proposed.
- **A tap in the minute after the day changes.** On a plan still on the screen from the day
  before, a change to one of its exercises does nothing but show today's plan.
- **A lift in front that keeps its sets through a check-in:** one the coach added, one standing in
  for a stopped lift, and one whose sets, reps or weight you set by hand, its effort included: a
  low check-in adds no rep in reserve to it.
- **An entered max rebuilds a lift's ramp sets by the rule** (older than this round): a ramp added
  by hand goes.
- **A superset whose first move is in front loses its pairing** on a check-in rebuild (older than
  this round).
- **A route's deload step is not recorded when the plan deloads by itself.** The card is left out
  that day, and the step comes back the next session that is not lighter, so a deload can follow
  the plan's own lighter day; a lift trained only every three weeks or more is lighter every time,
  and its route waits at that step. A deload week's sessions also count toward a route step
  (older). Proposed.
- **The row cap counts rows already done** (older than this round): once lifts are done, a
  rebuild can leave moves out while minutes remain (a check-in on a 60-minute push day three lifts
  in used 9 of 24 minutes), and a check-in back to fine does not bring them back. Proposed.
- **Loading a saved workout** (older than this round) brings back the sets of the day it was saved
  and none of today's settings: a deload week, a coach focus, a check-in, missing plates. Proposed.
- **A route step that opens a sheet** (a variation, a different exercise) is recorded as the sheet
  opens, even if you close it without a swap (older than this round).
- **A set given back can leave no slack for a later change:** a lift taken back to its 4 sets
  and kept in front can run over after a later change of place (8 of 40,000 chains checked).
- **A lift under way keeps its deload line through new weights or a new place.** In a deload week
  "loads 10% lighter" can be missing on a lift now lighter, or stay on one now at the plain load
  (444 and 484 of 9,360 changes checked), and "Why this workout" follows the lifts' lines. Lifts
  not begun are always right. Proposed.
- **An exact end time can be passed by up to 0.6 minutes** (older than this round): the week's
  order is applied after the fit, and giving sets back fills the time more closely, so it happens a
  little more often (6,036 of 127,200 rebuilds, against 4,892 without giving sets back and 8,064
  before this round). Proposed.
- **An entered max can take a plan well over its length at light dumbbells** (older than this
  round): lifts never logged move to the heaviest pair at many reps (75 minutes for an hour at
  dumbbells to 30 lb). The plan says how far over, and a change of length fits it again. Proposed.
- **A check-in rebuilds the lifts after the one in front** (older than this round), so a set taken
  off or added by hand to one of them comes back or goes.
- **A route's deload step waits on a day the plan steps a lift down** for the session or your
  habit, or at a new rep range, even where the load is not lighter than last time; it comes back
  the next session.
- **At a place with its dumbbells listed, a target between them is pushed** (52.5 lb where they go
  by 5 shows 50 lb for more reps), and a pushed set is offered no step up.
- **After "Take 65 lb today", a stalled lift's route can offer "Deload to 60 lb"** from the weight
  set by hand (older than this round).
- **The Plan tab says "loads 10% lighter" for a deload week** (older than this round), also at a
  place where the loads cannot go lighter. Proposed.
- **Two coach changes in flight at once** can save one's route step with the other's; the overlay
  keeps a second tap out.
- **In a check-in rebuild, a lift you pinned can lose a set to the time,** as a lift not begun
  does; in every other rebuild a pin keeps its sets.
- **After two sessions at the top and then a missed floor,** the plan repeats the load and a
  beginner's coach still offers "Take N" (older than this round, as before).
- **In kg, with dumbbells by 2.5 kg, the plan's 2 kg step lands back on the same pair** (older):
  the plan holds and the coach offers no step up, at every weight there.
- **An end time already past** on a workout not started still caps a change of length at 5
  minutes (older than this round); only a plan built again (for a coach focus, a deload week or a
  kept swap stopped) drops it.
- **A route's new rep range keeps the weight** (older than this round). The tap changes the reps
  and leaves the weight, and on a set a little short of its load the set keeps standing in for the
  old load and range, as reps set by hand on such a set do. Proposed.
- The exercise the coach adds now passes the place's weights to the set builder like every other
  call; with no ramp or drop set on it and its target already fitted, this changes nothing today,
  so it has no revert. Likewise the refill's check that a move comes back only once has no case in
  the plans checked; it stays so a move can never appear twice.

## Things to settle

1. Circuits on push and upper days at 30 minutes: the circuit's three moves (fly, pushdown,
   lateral raise) are all trained by the presses, so the rule leaves the whole circuit out first
   and brings one move back. Keep that, or shrink a circuit by its lowest member before leaving it
   out? Pull days keep theirs.
2. A main lift's secondary muscles count as trained (the rows train the biceps and rear delts), as
   in the design's example of a curl on a pull day. Counting primary muscles only would keep the
   rear-delt fly and drop the shrugs in your pull-day case. Keep it as built?

## Proposed

- **Add "Make it harder" and "Make it easier" to the workout.** The engine has the setting and now
  keeps it through every change, but no screen offers it. Before it ships, the short-session fit
  needs one more step: with harder on, a move that does not fit at its planned sets is left out
  whole, so a 15- or 30-minute session can end with less work than without harder (a fresh
  15-minute plan with harder is the main lift alone at 5 sets); it should try the move at fewer
  sets first. Harder can also take a kept lift below the sets it had (the bench press in front from
  4 to 3 at 45 minutes while the accessories go up): the fit should never trim a kept lift under
  its sets before harder.
- **Move up at a gap in the weights once the reps reach the top,** so a lift at the 50s moves to
  the 60s instead of holding 50 for good.
- **Make a deload week lighten even light loads by at least one step.**
- **Drop an end time once it has passed on a workout not started,** for every change, not only
  a plan built again.
- **Let a route's new rep range set its own weight,** and stand in for the new range rather than
  the old.
- **Let the app's day follow your own clock,** so a plan you have not started keeps your choices
  until midnight where you are, not 8 pm.
- **Count the plan's own lighter day as a route's deload step,** and keep a deload week's sessions
  out of a route step's count.
- **Count only rows still to do against the row cap,** so a rebuild after the main lift can use
  its minutes.
- **Load a saved workout with today's settings,** and save workouts without the day's own
  adjustments.
- **Record a route step that opens a sheet when its swap lands,** not when the sheet opens.
- **Judge the deload line again on a lift under way** when new weights or a new place refit it,
  keeping the plain load on the plan.
- **Apply the week's order before the fit to time,** so an exact end time is never passed.
- **Fit the plan to its length again after a max,** at light dumbbells above all.
- **Say "where the weights allow" on the Plan tab's deload line.**
