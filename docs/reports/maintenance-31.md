## The week model

One picture of the lifter's week, Monday to Sunday on the device, per muscle:

- the sets done this week so far, and of those, today's;
- the sets the week's sessions still to come will give, as the plan would build them, one after
  another, at Default length;
- the goal's weekly sets for the muscle;
- the days since the muscle was last trained.

A set counts in full for each of its exercise's main muscles and half for each secondary one, as
the weekly volume already does (Pelland et al. 2026). A warm-up counts for nothing, and a set not
done counts for nothing. A muscle counts as trained today only with sets for it directly: one a
lift touched as a secondary muscle was not trained today.

The sessions still to come are the days ticked after today, and today while it is ticked and no
workout was begun. With more days ticked than sessions a week, only as many as the week has left.

A muscle is **behind** when the sets done and still planned fall short of its weekly target: the
week's projected shortfall, never a share of it reached so far. Early in the week nothing is behind
that the week's own sessions will still give (the settled question 2).

## 12. A catch-up session for an unplanned day

**As agreed:** "On a day you didn't tick, Today says so in one line and offers a shorter catch-up
session built only from the muscles behind this week. The full session stays one tap away."

**Delivered:**

- On a day not ticked, Today says so in one line above the plan, for example "Not one of your
  training days: a 25-minute catch-up for what is behind this week." The button beside it, **Full
  session**, gives the usual plan; there, **Catch-up instead** gives the catch-up back. The choice
  holds for the day.
- It is the next length the app offers below the Default: 45 minutes for a Default of an hour
  (product judgement: the owner asked for a shorter session, and the evidence sets no length). A
  length the lifter picks stands.
- The catch-up is built only from the muscles behind this week, each with its own main lift, the
  strength lifts first, the largest share behind first. Muscles share a lift where one lift there
  works them both (the squat for the quads and the glutes, a hammer curl for the biceps and the
  forearms), at the fewer sets either is short; where none does, each takes a lift of its own (a
  wrist curl and a curl). Every lift works a muscle it went in for directly: a favourite, or a
  lasting swap, that does not waits for another day (a front squat works the glutes only at half, so
  a glutes catch-up takes the back squat), and so does a lasting swap to a lift for fewer of the
  muscles a lift was shared by. A lift the plan never picks, a lift swapped out for weeks or the
  lift to push off the plan, gives no muscle its slot. Every lift that works a muscle directly
  counts against its sets, so no muscle gets more than it lacks, through another muscle's lift
  included, a lift begun before a rebuild, or one picked up again back at the gym.
- No lift takes fewer than two working sets (at least two sets an exercise, ACSM 2026): a muscle
  with one set left waits for the week's own sessions, and no other lift takes it either.
- A muscle whose own lift is out takes the next one that carries it: once the squat has given the
  glutes their sets, every hinge works the glutes, so the hamstrings take the leg curl. Where every
  lift of the split for a muscle works one that rests, a lift that leads with it comes in: the hip
  thrust or the bridge for the glutes.
- Each muscle gets 3 working sets, or 2 for a beginner (the settled question 1). Never more than
  the week is short, nor than the muscle's sets in one of the plan's own sessions, and never fewer
  than 2: a muscle short by less than 2 sets is left for the week's own sessions.
- No set to failure: at least 1 rep in reserve, 2 for a beginner.
- A muscle trained in the last 48 hours sits out, as on any day (the settled question 3: the rule
  is unchanged). No lift in the catch-up works one directly either: the glutes behind, with the
  quads trained yesterday, take the Romanian deadlift rather than a squat. A muscle whose every lift
  would work one waits for the week's own sessions, and the plan names only the muscles it caught
  up.
- Off the plan, in a catch-up or a second workout: no lift to push in any form (its own place, the
  check set, the extra session), no drop set, and the coach offers nothing that adds work (a set, a
  drop set, a longer range, an exercise). A catch-up is the week's missing sets, and the push waits
  for a planned day. The full session, one tap away, is the usual plan in full.
- No swap brings in a lift for a muscle the day leaves alone: not a kept swap, nor the one
  **Hurts**, **Uncomfortable** or a place that cannot do a lift makes.
- "Why this workout" says what it is for: "A catch-up for what this week still lacks: side delts
  and chest, a few sets each and none to failure."
- Who is offered one: a lifter in training, with a workout in the last two weeks. A new lifter, or
  one back after a break, has no week to catch up yet and gets the full session on any day
  (product judgement). Nor in a deload week: the deload's lighter week stands. On such a day,
  Today still says "Not one of your training days.", with nothing to choose.
- A schedule, an experience or a place saved reads the day's offer again for a plan not yet
  started: tick today and the catch-up gives way to the usual plan, untick it and the catch-up
  comes, built whole, as the full session taken is: a swap or a pin made on the one plan stays with
  it. So is a plan not begun at a new place, or with new equipment at the place, where the day's
  catch-up had no lift where the plan was made: the new place may have one. A workout already begun
  keeps its offer.
- A catch-up leaves the split's rotation as it was: the next planned day plans as if the catch-up
  had held none of the split's sessions.
- With nothing behind, the line says so ("Not one of your training days, and nothing is behind this
  week.") and the plan is the usual one, as before the round. So too when nothing behind can be
  caught up today, each lift for it working a muscle trained in the last two days ("Not one of your
  training days, and nothing behind this week can be caught up today.").

## 32. A second workout on the same day

**As agreed:** "A second workout the same day should lead with what you missed ... and go easy on
what you already did."

**Delivered:**

- On a day a workout was begun and a working set of it done, the next plan is a second workout (a
  workout begun by mistake and ended with nothing logged is no first workout). Today says so in one
  line: "You trained today: this one leads with what you missed and goes easy on the rest."
- It leads with the muscles behind this week that were not trained today and are outside the 48
  hours, dosed as a catch-up's, their strength lifts first; what is left of today's work comes after
  them.
- A muscle trained today takes at most what is left of the dose today's own session gave it, three
  or more reps short of failure, and only while the week is still short (the settled question 4).
  The dose is the day's first workout as it was planned, each set counted as the week counts the
  sets done, a lift that works the muscle only secondarily at half: a workout done in full leaves
  nothing, one cut short what it did not do, and a deload day or a shorter session no more than it
  planned. In the usual case, a full first session, nothing is left, and it sits out. A muscle
  trained in the 48 hours before today as well takes nothing more (the 48-hour rule). A beginner's
  muscles trained today always sit out (the settled question 5).
- When nothing missed could go in, only what is left of today's, the line says so: "You trained
  today: this one goes easy, with only what is left of today's work."
- In a deload week a second workout is still offered, going easy on what was done.
- A muscle that sits out gets no lift that works it directly, through another muscle's lift
  included, and the coach offers no work for it. A lift two muscles share, one of them trained
  today, takes the lighter dose: what is left for that one, at its reserve, three or more short of
  failure, with no drop set.
- "Why this workout" says so: "A second workout today: it leads with what you missed, chest.",
  "Quads take only what is left of one session, three or more reps short of failure.",
  "Hamstrings, trained today, sit out."
- With nothing behind, the plan is the usual one without what was trained today: "You trained
  today, and nothing is behind this week: what you trained sits out." Never a second full dose for a
  muscle trained today.
- When every lift left works a muscle trained today (a full-body session done, say), there is
  nothing to start: "You trained today, and nothing is behind this week: nothing more for today."
  The **Full session** is one tap away, the lifter's to choose, and **Go easy instead** gives the
  day's own plan back.

## How it is kept

- The day's offer is part of the plan kept for the day (the session's constraints), as the
  readiness check and the swaps are. Two new fields, each optional and dropped alone when an older
  or damaged copy cannot read it, never the session: the catch-up's muscles, and whether the full
  session was taken. Their kinds and reasons are the engine's own lists, not a copy. The plan keeps
  its catch-up's slots too, optional the same way: a plan without them lays its catch-up out again.
- A change to the plan (a swap, a length, a rebuild) keeps the day's offer and the lifter's choice,
  and holds every lift to it: a check-in, "Make it harder" or "easier", whatever comes in for a lift
  ("Hurts", "Uncomfortable", a swap), the place's weights or a plate missing today, a max, a change
  of place, busy equipment, and a lift picked up again back at the gym keep a catch-up's sets and
  reserve. "Make it harder" takes a lift no further than a plan made harder would: a set more only
  where its muscles have room for one beside the plan's other lifts. The catch-up's shorter length
  holds wherever the length is read: a check-in short on time, an end time, a rebuild under way. A
  plan built again from scratch reads the day's offer afresh, and the full session or the catch-up
  taken again is another plan, built whole. A plan rebuilt around what it keeps (a pin, a workout
  begun) is rebuilt from the offer as it was built: a workout begun on the split's session, where
  the catch-up had no lift, stays that session at the next place. A catch-up keeps the slots it was
  laid out in with the plan, as the usual plan keeps its template: a rebuild around what it keeps
  fills the same slots, so every lift kept stays in its own, at a new place too; a plan built whole
  lays them out again from what the place has. A muscle a slot's lift misses, still short, takes a
  slot of its own, numbered after the layout's so that every lift keeps its name, and placed in the
  plan right after that slot (a front squat for the quads in the squat they share with the glutes,
  at a place with no squat for both), unless a swap the lifter made there left it so, or the slot
  has no lift going on (a stand-in skipped brings no sets back). The full session taken holds for
  its own day only.

## Known limits

- **The week is this device's.** Workouts logged on another device, or not logged, are not in it,
  so a muscle trained that way can read as behind. The sessions still to come are read as the plan
  would build them today at Default length: one later made shorter, or swapped, gives the week less
  than it counted on.
- **A catch-up leaves the split's rotation as it was.** The next planned day plans as if the
  catch-up held none of the split's sessions, so a muscle caught up on a Wednesday is trained again
  on its own day, where the 48-hour rule holds as on any day.
- **The glutes ride with the squat.** A glutes catch-up takes a squat, which works the quads too,
  as the lower day's own squat does: a muscle not behind can take sets that way.
- **Muscles that every lift works together.** Every rear-delt lift works the upper back directly,
  so once the upper back's row has given it its sets, no rear-delt lift keeps the upper back within
  what it lacks, and the rear delts wait for the week's own sessions. One rear-delt fly would serve
  both when they are the only two; with the lats behind too it would only move the loss to them.
  The rule that no muscle gets more than it lacks decides it.
- **A closed slot is the plan's, not the session's.** A catch-up with a slot closed and lifts left,
  taken to the full session and back, is laid out again: the lifts the lifter turned away today stay

  out stays out.
- **A saved workout takes its turn in the split, even with nothing logged.** As every saved workout
  has, it moves the split on: the full session after it is the next in turn, and the day's catch-up
  is read again with the week it leaves, so a plan emptied for the old offer is not the new one's.
- **A save that changes the day's offer builds the new plan whole.** A swap or a pin made on the

- **What is left of today's work is read from the first workout's plan.** A first workout planned
  shorter (a length the lifter picked, a deload day) leaves no more than it planned, though the

- **A saved catch-up is itself on any day.** Loaded, it is built for its own muscles, so one trained

- **The evidence is thin on a second dose the same day.** No trial has given a muscle a second full
